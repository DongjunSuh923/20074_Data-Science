# Strategic Insights: Cross-Family Comparison

This note summarizes the first cross-family comparison across the current official scenario set:

- `Capacity`
  - `snow_heat`
  - `tornado`
  - `wildfire_smoke`
- `Commodity-specific supply`
  - `grain_supply`
- `Demand`
  - `construction_hybrid`
  - `domestic_bottleneck`
  - `domestic_broad_demand`
- `Trade/external network`
  - `trade_external_enhanced`

## 1. Which states are the most consistently repeated?

The most repeated non-must-have candidates are:
- `WA`
- `MN`
- `NC`
- `NE`
- `CO`
- `KS`

These are the strongest cross-family survivors because they appear across **all four scenario families** and remain visible across many individual scenarios.

Immediately behind them are:
- `OR`
- `WI`
- `VA`
- `AL`

These are still very strong, but either appear in slightly fewer scenarios or with slightly weaker rank consistency.

## 2. What does that mean structurally?

The repeated candidates naturally split into three broad roles.

### A. Western / Pacific support
- `WA`
- `OR`
- `CO`

These states repeatedly survive because they support either:
- Pacific gateway functions
- western fallback routing
- or interior balancing when coastal/core states are stressed

### B. Plains / Midwest balancing support
- `MN`
- `NE`
- `KS`
- `WI`

These states repeatedly survive because they help stabilize the interior network.
They are especially strong when:
- grain supply is stressed
- construction/material demand shifts
- or inland redistribution becomes more important

### C. East / Southeast reinforcement
- `NC`
- `VA`
- `AL`

These states repeatedly survive because they support:
- East Coast corridor reliability
- southeastern growth-belt demand
- inland distribution reinforcement

## 3. Which states are the strongest scenario-only signals?

Among non-NGL, non-must-have states, the strongest repeated signals are:
- `NC`
- `VA`

These are the two cleanest cross-family reinforcement candidates because they:
- appear across all four families
- survive across multiple specific shocks
- and were already repeatedly visible in the family-by-family interpretation work

Below them are:
- `UT`
- `SC`
- `ND`
- `NM`
- `NJ`

These are still useful, but they are more conditional:
- `UT` and `NM` are more fallback/interior-specific
- `SC` is more demand/trade-sensitive
- `ND` is more grain/construction-sensitive
- `NJ` is very strong where it appears, but it is concentrated in fewer families

## 4. Which non-must-have NGL states never survive?

The non-must-have states in the current NGL footprint that never appear in any included scenario are:
- `AZ`
- `CT`
- `KY`
- `MA`
- `MO`
- `RI`

This does **not** automatically mean those states are poor assets.
It means that under the current state-level resilience lens, they do not emerge as repeated reinforcement candidates.

So this list is better interpreted as:
- `lower-priority reinforcement states`
rather than
- `states that should definitely be removed`

## 5. Practical working interpretation

At this stage, the clearest cross-family messages are:

### Most robust repeated support set
- `WA, MN, NC, NE, CO, KS`

### Strong second-line repeated support set
- `OR, WI, VA, AL`

### Strongest non-NGL reinforcement signals
- `NC`
- `VA`

### Lower-priority non-must-have NGL states under the resilience lens
- `AZ, CT, KY, MA, MO, RI`

## 6. Why this matters for the next step

This comparison is important because it distinguishes:
- `family-specific candidates`
from
- `true cross-family reinforcement candidates`

In other words:
- `NC` and `VA` are not just domestic-shock anomalies anymore
- `WA`, `MN`, `NE`, `KS`, and `CO` are not just single-family artifacts either

They are the states that keep surviving even when the type of stress changes.
